1⍝# Comparison: the Rosetta stone's state machine (docs/rosetta.md), a 2⍝# library beside the demo ("cm:" u_se< "Comparison"). The state is one 3⍝# 4 x 5 matrix of numbers. The first three rows are the axes (the 4⍝# idiom drum, the top ring, the bottom ring), four columns each: how 5⍝# many items the axis has, how many steps it has taken (an integer; the 6⍝# item facing is 1 + steps, wrapped, and this is the truth), its angle 7⍝# in degrees (90 per step; the presentation, easing toward 90 * steps) 8⍝# and whether it plays on its own. The fourth row is the pointer: the 9⍝# axis being dragged (0 none, 0.5 pressed and not yet moved, else the 10⍝# axis), where it last was (x, y), how far it has moved in all, and the 11⍝# last turn it gave in degrees (for a flick). The fifth row is the 12⍝# attract mode's clock: seconds since the last interaction, seconds 13⍝# since its last step, and its two counters (how far the bottom ring 14⍝# is through its sweep of the other languages, how far the drum is 15⍝# through its sweep of the idioms). Nothing 16⍝# is ever inferred from an angle except what to draw. Every function 17⍝# gives a new state; none has an effect. Events (RS1) come in as 18⍝# []E_VENT gives them; cm:u_pdate takes one. 19 20⍝# The attract mode's pace, in seconds: how long a comparison holds 21⍝# still before the next (long enough to read two faces of code), and 22⍝# how long after an interaction the stone waits before moving on its 23⍝# own again (long enough to look round a comparison by hand).
