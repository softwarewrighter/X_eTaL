X_eTaL is an array language: a name like r_ev is a function, and it works on a whole array at once. You type plain ASCII, and X_eTaL draws what you typed with the decorations that tell you what each name is. Each block below is a line you could type into xetal repl or the live demo; above it is how X_eTaL draws it, and below it what it prints.

Typing ASCII, seeing it drawn

An underscore after the first letter makes a name a function: r_ev is drawn as rev with the r underlined, and it reverses. 

r̲ev "stressed"

⍝ typed:
⍝   r_ev "stressed"
desserts

The underline is the whole difference between a value and a function: rev would be an ordinary variable. The editor and the REPL draw each line as you type it, so you always see which is which.

Arrays

Arithmetic works on whole arrays. A single number on one side meets every item on the other. 

1 2 3 + 10

⍝ typed:
⍝   1 2 3 + 10
11 12 13

r_ange 6 is 1 to 6, and r_eshape makes a matrix of it: the shape goes on the left. 

m ← 2 3 r̲eshape r̲ange 6
m

⍝ typed:
⍝   m := 2 3 r_eshape r_ange 6
⍝   m
1 2 3
4 5 6

:= binds a name; it is drawn as an arrow.

Functions and operands

A function passed to another function is quoted. '+ r_/ is "reduce by plus": the sum. On a matrix it adds the rows together, giving one sum per column. 

'+ r̲/ 1 2 3 4
'+ r̲/ m

⍝ typed:
⍝   '+ r_/ 1 2 3 4
⍝   '+ r_/ m
10
5 7 9

Your own functions are lambdas in braces, _r standing for the right argument (drawn as APL's omega). A name of yours carries the u: prefix, drawn as a small superscript. 

ᵘs̲quare ← { ⍵ × ⍵ }
ᵘs̲quare 1 2 3
'ᵘs̲quare e̲ach 4 5

⍝ typed:
⍝   u:s_quare := { _r * _r }
⍝   u:s_quare 1 2 3
⍝   'u:s_quare e_ach 4 5
1 4 9
16 25

An axis

A function works along the first axis unless a subscript names another. '+ r_/_2 m adds along axis 2, within each row; 1 o_-_2 m rotates each row by one. 

'+ r̲/₂ m
1 o̲-₂ m

⍝ typed:
⍝   '+ r_/_2 m
⍝   1 o_-_2 m
6 15
2 3 1
5 6 4

A train

Functions in square brackets make a new function without naming its argument. Three are a fork: ['+ r_/ / t_ally] x is the sum of x divided by its count, the mean. Two are an atop: [n_eg a_bs] x is n_eg (a_bs x). 

ᵘm̲ean ← ['+ r̲/ ÷ t̲ally]
ᵘm̲ean 1 2 3 4
[n̲eg a̲bs] -5

⍝ typed:
⍝   u:m_ean := ['+ r_/ / t_ally]
⍝   u:m_ean 1 2 3 4
⍝   [n_eg a_bs] -5
2.5
-5

A library

u_se< imports a library under a prefix of your choice; its names then carry that prefix. Stats is one of the libraries built in. 

ˢ⁼u̲se< "Stats"
ˢs̲d 2 4 4 4 5 5 7 9

⍝ typed:
⍝   "s:" u_se< "Stats"
⍝   s:s_d 2 4 4 4 5 5 7 9
2.0
