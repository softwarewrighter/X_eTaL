# Classic APL programs in X_eTaL

The programs APL has been taught and shown off with for decades,
written as X_eTaL says them. Each is a commented notebook file in
`demos/classics/`: run one with `just show demos/classics/NAME.xtl`
to see every line beside its output, or `just run FILE` for the output
alone. Each is also in the live demo's Open list, and its output is
pinned by a golden (`reg/run-classics-NAME`), so the programs keep
working as the language grows.

Some programs also draw. `[]G_RID` turns an array into a picture (an
SVG document: a matrix as a grid of cells, a rank-3 array as frames
shown in turn) and `[]S_HOW` shows it: `just draw FILE` runs the
program and opens its first picture in the browser, and `xetal run
--draw DIR FILE` writes every picture to DIR as `NAME-1.svg`,
`NAME-2.svg`, and so on. Several of the programs are also walked
through, with their pictures, in the literate document
[classics.org](literate/classics.org).

| Program | Why it is a classic | Concepts | File |
| ------- | ------------------- | -------- | ---- |
| Pascal's triangle | The quintessential array construction: a whole row at a time, no loop over items | shift and add by rotate, function power, stacking rows with `c_at`, binomials by `t_able`, text layout with `t_ake`, Sierpinski's triangle modulo 2, both drawn with `[]G_RID`; ragged rows as boxes by `m_ap` | [pascal.xtl](../demos/classics/pascal.xtl) |
| Sieve of Eratosthenes | The classic demonstration of eliminating explicit loops | remainders with `m_od`, masks, `w_here` and `s_elect` to keep what a prime does not divide, recursion that stops at the square root | [sieve.xtl](../demos/classics/sieve.xtl) |
| Primes by divisors | Very compact and recognizable: every question asked of a table at once | the remainder table (`'m_od t_able`), counting divisors by reduce along axis 2, perfect numbers | [primes.xtl](../demos/classics/primes.xtl) |
| Euclid's GCD | Historically present in APL examples since APL\\360 | a guard and a recursion, a dyadic function as the operand of reduce and each, the least common multiple | [gcd.xtl](../demos/classics/gcd.xtl) |
| Fibonacci | A simple recurrence: the contrast between scalar and array thinking | the recursive definition run by each, a vector grown from its last two items, powers of a matrix by inner product | [fibonacci.xtl](../demos/classics/fibonacci.xtl) |
| Factorial and combinations | Tiny but mathematically recognizable | factorial as a product reduction, combinations and permutations, e as a sum of 1/k! | [factorial.xtl](../demos/classics/factorial.xtl) |
| Collatz sequence | A small iterative problem, then asked of many starts at once | a predicate in a guard, a recursion building the sequence, steps for 1 to 30 by each, `i_ndexOf` the longest | [collatz.xtl](../demos/classics/collatz.xtl) |
| Tower of Hanoi | Where scalar, recursive thinking stays natural, even in APL, and where the array way is a surprise | the moves by recursion, pegs permuted by `s_elect`; every move at once from the trailing zeros of the move number (a remainder table), checked with `m_atch`; the state moved by `i_ndexOf` and a mask and drawn as frames with `[]G_RID`; three ways in [hanoi.org](literate/hanoi.org) | [hanoi.xtl](../demos/classics/hanoi.xtl) |
| Quicksort | The famous recursive APL definition | partitions as masks (`v < p`), selection by `w_here` and `s_elect`, recursion on each side; checked against `s_ort`, with `g_rade` beside it | [quicksort.xtl](../demos/classics/quicksort.xtl) |
| Matrix multiplication | APL's inner product, in one expression | `'+ '* i_nner`, an identity by cyclic `r_eshape`, walks counted by powers of an adjacency matrix, any two functions as an inner product (`'+ '=`) | [matmul.xtl](../demos/classics/matmul.xtl) |
| Transitive closure and Warshall | A graph algorithm that is all array operations | the Boolean inner product `'\| '& i_nner` repeated until nothing changes; Warshall's vertex-by-vertex update as a column `'& t_able` a row; the two checked equal, and Warshall drawn as frames | [closure.xtl](../demos/classics/closure.xtl) |
| Shortest paths | Min-plus algebra: the generalized inner product as a graph algorithm | `'m_in '+ i_nner` repeated (Floyd); widest paths by `'m_ax 'm_in`; distances drawn on the palette | [shortest.xtl](../demos/classics/shortest.xtl) |
| Polynomials, differences, moving averages | Numerical computation where APL's style shines | Horner's rule as a right-fold reduction, every value at once; finite differences by dropping one end (constant for a polynomial at its degree); moving averages by a table of windows and by differences of a running sum | [sequences.xtl](../demos/classics/sequences.xtl) |
| Cellular automata | Life's one-dimensional cousin: every cell updated at once | a rule's bits by `d_iv` and `m_od`, each cell's case from its neighbours by rotation, the history grown row by row; rules 30, 90 (Sierpinski) and 110 drawn | [automaton.xtl](../demos/classics/automaton.xtl) |
| Histogram | A tiny practical array program | distinct values by `u_nique`, counts by a table of equals summed along its rows, most frequent first by grading, bars as text by a table of `>=`; letter counts drawn | [histogram.xtl](../demos/classics/histogram.xtl) |
| Sorting, grading, duplicates | APL's separation of grade from indexing | `g_rade` then `s_elect`, sorting one thing by another (names by age), ranks as the grade of the grade; duplicates removed by `u_nique` and by the mask where `i_ndexOf` finds an item's own place | [sorting.xtl](../demos/classics/sorting.xtl) |
| Run-length encoding | An excellent idiom exercise | each item against the one before, `w_here` the runs start, the items there, the lengths as differences of the starts; decoding as one `r_eplicate`, checked by a round trip; the longest run of heads | [rle.xtl](../demos/classics/rle.xtl) |
| Magic squares | A favourite of recreational mathematics, and of APL's | the Siamese square for any odd n as two cyclic Latin squares, each a list of rotations (`o_-` with a list of amounts); every row, column and diagonal checked by reductions and an identity table; a doubly even square by complement, giving Durer's 1514 square; drawn on the palette | [magic.xtl](../demos/classics/magic.xtl) |
| Mastermind | Scoring a guess is a neat count-and-compare idiom | black pegs by `=` summed, colours in common by counts (`'= t_able`) and `m_in`; all 1296 codes as base-6 digits by `d_iv` and `m_od`; one guess scored against every code at once; a player that keeps only the codes still possible; a game against the computer with typed guesses (`[]R_EAD`) | [mastermind.xtl](../demos/classics/mastermind.xtl), [mastermind-play.xtl](../demos/classics/mastermind-play.xtl) |
| Truth tables | Boolean logic over every case at once | the table as the binary digits of 0 to 2^n - 1 by `e_ncode`; an expression of its rows evaluated for every column; laws checked by and-reduction; the columns where a function is true by `r_eplicate_2`; a full adder; a function's number (rule 30) by `d_ecode` | [truth.xtl](../demos/classics/truth.xtl) |
| Base conversion | Encode and decode are APL's own radix tools | binary, octal and hexadecimal; just the digits needed, by an or-scan mask and `r_eplicate`; hex as text and back by `s_elect` and `i_ndexOf`; mixed radixes (days, hours, minutes, seconds; yards, feet, inches); Gray code | [bases.xtl](../demos/classics/bases.xtl) |
| Roman numerals | A favourite small conversion, both ways | decimal digits by `e_ncode`, each digit a pattern of its place's three letters, the letters kept by `r_eplicate`; read back by each letter's value with a sign from the next; every number from 1 to 3999 round-tripped | [roman.xtl](../demos/classics/roman.xtl) |
| Magmas: rock-paper-scissors (and lizard, Spock) | A game as an algebra: which laws hold, checked over every case at once | the winner as a binary operation from a rule modulo 3 (or 5), its Cayley table by `t_able`, shown by name with nested strings; commutative and idempotent but not associative (every triple by `e_ncode`), so the order of play matters (right and left folds differ); addition and subtraction modulo 3 for comparison; who beats whom, each move beating exactly two | [magmas.xtl](../demos/magmas.xtl) |
| Word frequency | The classic first program with nested arrays | words by `p_artition` on the mask of non-blanks, distinct words by `u_nique` on boxes, counts by a table of equals, most frequent first by grading, a two-row table of boxes | [wordfreq.xtl](../demos/classics/wordfreq.xtl) |
| N-Queens | A search the array way: every partial placement grown at once | each placement tried with every column (`r_eplicate` the rows, `c_at_2` the columns beside them), the attacked ones dropped by a mask; 92 solutions for 8; boards as boxed Char matrices by `p_artition` and `m_ap`, all 92 drawn | [queens.xtl](../demos/classics/queens.xtl) |
| A tiny APL | An APL interpreter is a classic exercise, and one written in an array language reads like its own grammar | tokens cut by `p_artition` from a running sum of where each token starts; right-to-left evaluation, a function taking everything to its right and the operand next to it on its left; parentheses found by a running depth count; dyadic `+ - * % max min`, monadic `- i r`, reductions, `pick` and `iota`; operands passed in boxes; sessions where `)ORIGIN 0` or `)ORIGIN 1` changes the index origin and answers what it WAS, as in APL\360; an experimental (joke) flag that also allows 0.5, where counting starts at a half and a whole position blends two items | [mini-apl.xtl](../demos/classics/mini-apl.xtl) |
| Conway's Life | The best-known modern APL demo, in one line | rotations over two axes, reduce over two axes, Boolean arithmetic | [life.xtl](../demos/life.xtl) |
| Turtle graphics | Logo's turtle, without the turtle: the classic fractals as running sums | a walk as a vector of turns, positions by scans of `c_os` and `s_in` (the Turtle library), Koch's snowflake and Sierpinski's arrowhead by recursion, drawing a curve as it goes by selecting a growing prefix with `s_elect_2` | [turtle.xtl](../demos/classics/turtle.xtl), [lib/Turtle.xtl](../lib/Turtle.xtl) |
| Mandelbrot set | Small but visually rewarding: every point of the plane iterated at once | z as two Float arrays (real and imaginary), the state as planes of a rank-3 array, iteration by `p_ower`, escaped points frozen by a mask; a still, a zoom and a fly-over drawn as images | [mandelbrot.xtl](../demos/classics/mandelbrot.xtl) |
| Life, drawn | Life is best watched | generations stacked as frames (rank 3), animated with `[]G_RID`; a torus by rotation, a box by a mask of dead border cells | [life-drawn.xtl](../demos/classics/life-drawn.xtl) |
| Swimming ducks | Ducks are iconic in graphics programming, and animation by rotation is pure APL | a character matrix slid along axis 2 by `o_-_2`, wrapping round; a list of amounts giving every frame at once as a rank-3 array; waves rotated the other way and joined under the ducks by `c_at_2`; drawn and animated with `[]G_RID`; the workaround and the join compared in [duck.org](literate/duck.org) | [duck.xtl](../demos/classics/duck.xtl) |
| Tic-tac-toe | A complete application rather than an expression puzzle: a machine that learns to play | boards as vectors, symmetry by indexing, inner product over lines, files and the keyboard | [tttml.xtl](../demos/tttml.xtl), [lib/TTTML.xtl](../lib/TTTML.xtl) |
